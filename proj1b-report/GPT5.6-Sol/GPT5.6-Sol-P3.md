## Social-leaderboard verdict

**DIFFERENTIATOR—with an adjacent abandonment warning.**

No verified general food-delivery marketplace currently combines all four proposed elements: friend connections, order-derived loyalty points, badges, and personal donation impact. It is not entirely unprecedented, however:

- Chipotle’s 2026 Summer of Extras used purchase streaks, badges, and local/state/national leaderboards, but not friend-only or donation rankings. [Chipotle](https://ir.chipotle.com/2026-05-28-CHIPOTLES-SUMMER-OF-EXTRAS-RETURNS,-WITH-STREAK-BASED-REWARDS-AND-LOCAL-RANKINGS-TO-GAMIFY-THE-REWARDS-EXPERIENCE)
- BetterPoints currently combines reward points, user-created friends/family leaderboards, and charitable point donations—the closest mechanical analogue outside food delivery. [BetterPoints](https://betterpointsltd.zendesk.com/hc/en-gb/articles/18283109163921-What-are-leaderboards)
- Beli uses food-related user and school leaderboards, but ranks restaurant-logging activity rather than verified purchases. [Beli](https://www.foodnetwork.com/fn-dish/news/beli-app-trend)
- Duolingo and Strava continue to operate weekly or club leaderboards, proving the pattern remains viable. [Duolingo](https://blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-leagues-leaderboards/), [Strava](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15402172-clubs-on-strava)
- Fitbit is the abandonment warning: it discontinued its social Challenges, Adventures, and trophies in March 2023. [Fitbit community announcement](https://community.fitbit.com/t5/Product-Feedback/Don-t-remove-Challenges-amp-Adventures/idc-p/5335847/highlight/true)

The proposed leaderboard is therefore differentiated, but the full friend system is risky for one month: it introduces requests, blocking, privacy settings, empty networks, moderation, score normalization, ties, resets, and abuse controls before the experiment can even test whether competition changes ordering behavior.

## Stronger one-month differentiator

A stronger next use case is an **order-level Meal-for-a-Meal Impact Receipt**:

> After each completed delivery, show “This order funded one meal,” the customer’s verified lifetime impact, progress toward the next impact badge, the participating restaurant/cause, and an optional share card.

This is stronger because it deepens Hungry Wolf’s distinctive mission instead of copying a familiar leaderboard, reuses UC6, UC9, UC19, and UC20, requires no friend graph or network critical mass, and can be built and tested within a month. The closest restaurant analogue is Beam, but its community donation tracking is still much less common than leaderboards.

Only describe a meal as “verified” if Hungry Wolf can reconcile completed-order records with an actual donation obligation; otherwise label it “committed” or “pledged.”

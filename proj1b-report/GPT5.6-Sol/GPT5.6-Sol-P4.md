The material points to a recovery-first release: with only one month to build and test, Hungry Wolf should not add autonomous ordering until passwords, payment authorization, and server-authoritative financial records are fixed. The feasible extension is a hosted, tokenized payment flow; a voice assistant that can prepare and modify a cart but must read back the final price and obtain explicit confirmation; optional, explainable menu personalization; and immutable server-side ledgers for orders, tips, partner pay, loyalty points, and donations.

Ratings are launch gates: “MUST-READ” means mandatory when the stated jurisdiction or feature applies, not that every regional law applies everywhere. This is a design reading register, not a legal opinion, and reflects sources available on September 13, 2026.

## The shortest defensible one-month reading packet

If the team cannot read every item immediately, these are the launch blockers:

1. FTC App Developers: Start with Security.
2. NIST SP 800-63B and the OWASP Password Storage Cheat Sheet.
3. OWASP API Security Top 10 and Transaction Authorization Cheat Sheet.
4. PCI DSS/SAQ A guidance.
5. FTC’s food-delivery fee ANPRM and .com Disclosures.
6. BBB Standard 19 plus the commercial-coventurer law for every launch state.
7. DOL worker-classification guidance plus the applicable California, NYC or Seattle rules.
8. WCAG 2.2, WCAG2Mobile, ADA guidance and W3C cognitive voice guidance.
9. The exact payment, voice-model, map and geocoding vendor terms selected for the build.
10. Applicable privacy and breach law—at minimum the launch state’s law, plus GDPR/CCPA when triggered.

Reading those materials would likely eliminate fully autonomous voice checkout from the one-month scope. A safer deliverable is “voice-assisted cart and order confirmation,” backed by a hosted payment processor and server-controlled ledgers, with autonomous tool use postponed until security, accessibility, payment-failure, duplicate-order, substitution, refund and dispute tests are complete.

The current identity and integrity layer is not fit for a social feature. A leaderboard would turn existing security bugs into privacy, fraud, consumer-protection, and reputational risks: attackers could impersonate users, forge donation impact, double-spend points, manipulate ranks, and alter worker pay. With only one month to build and test, the defensible next release is a “trust and integrity” release; the leaderboard should remain a private prototype using synthetic data until authentication, authorization, and auditable transaction controls pass adversarial tests.

Priorities below are scoped: a jurisdiction-specific source becomes MUST-READ when Hungry Wolf serves users or workers there. This is a design research map, not legal advice.

## What this material should change now

For the one-month release, the stronger use case is **verified impact and account integrity**, not a full friend-connected leaderboard:

1. Migrate plaintext passwords to Argon2id and invalidate or reset exposed credentials.
2. Add server-issued sessions, role-based authorization, and ownership checks on every protected endpoint.
3. Replace point balances with an atomic, append-only transaction ledger using idempotency keys and database constraints.
4. Derive delivery pay entirely from trusted server data, with bounded values, pay statements, audit history, and correction workflow.
5. Derive UC20 only from completed, non-refunded, server-verified orders and reconcile it against actual partner-charity transfers.
6. Add audit logs and a documented points/donation/pay dispute process.
7. Test concurrency, replay, impersonation, authorization, negative/oversized values, abuse rates, accessibility, and rollback.

If a leaderboard must be tested during the month, use a synthetic-data prototype or a tightly controlled pilot: affirmative opt-in, mutual friends only, pseudonymous display, private by default, granular metric visibility, weekly reset, no exact order or donation amounts, server-verified events only, daily scoring caps, an easy opt-out, report/block controls, an appeal route, and a feature kill switch. Do not rank users primarily by dollars spent or meals ordered; that is the version most likely to encourage unhealthy purchasing, expose economic differences, and turn the donation claim into status theater.
